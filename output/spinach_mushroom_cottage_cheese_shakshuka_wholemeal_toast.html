Spinach, Mushroom & Cottage Cheese Shakshuka with Wholemeal Toast

Recipe info

A protein-rich shakshuka with deeply browned mushrooms, spinach and a smoky cumin-tomato sauce, finished with softly set eggs, creamy cottage cheese and crisp wholemeal toast.

Mise en place

Prep 1

Peel the onion, trim the root and stem ends, then dice the usable flesh into approximately 5 mm pieces. Peel and finely grate or crush the garlic. Trim the mushroom stem ends and slice the mushrooms approximately 5 mm thick.

Ready when: The onion is evenly diced, the garlic is finely prepared and the mushrooms are sliced to a consistent thickness.

Prep 2

Wash the spinach if required, then dry it thoroughly. Remove only any unusually thick or damaged stems.

Ready when: The spinach is clean and dry enough that it will wilt without adding unnecessary water to the sauce.

Prep 3

Keep the 2 eggs whole until required. Weigh 200 g liquid egg whites and 160 g cottage cheese into separate ready-to-use portions.

Ready when: The eggs, egg whites and cottage cheese are ready to add without interrupting cooking.

Prep 4

Measure 1 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp ground cumin, 1/4 tsp chilli flakes, 1/4 tsp fine salt, 1/4 tsp black pepper, 1 tbsp tomato purée and 1 tsp red wine vinegar so they are ready to add in sequence.

Ready when: All seasoning and sauce ingredients are measured before cooking begins.

Method

Step 1

Brown the mushrooms and soften the onion

Vegetable base
Heat source: gas_hobSetting: medium-highTime: 7–9 min

Heat the sauté pan over medium-high heat for approximately 1 minute. Add 1 tsp olive oil, followed by the mushrooms and onion. Spread them across the pan and cook for approximately 7-9 minutes, stirring only every 60-90 seconds at first so the mushrooms can release their moisture and develop brown edges.

Ready when: The mushroom liquid has evaporated, the mushrooms are well browned and the onion is soft with lightly golden edges.
Corrective actions
  • If: The mushrooms release liquid and remain grey rather than browning.
    Do: Keep the pan uncovered at medium-high heat and avoid stirring until the visible liquid has evaporated.
  • If: The onion begins darkening before the mushrooms have browned.
    Do: Reduce the heat slightly and stir more frequently until the mushrooms catch up.
Step 2

Bloom the garlic, spices and tomato purée

Tomato sauce
Heat source: gas_hobSetting: mediumTime: 0.75–1 min

Reduce the heat to medium. Add the garlic, smoked paprika, cumin, chilli flakes and tomato purée. Stir continuously for approximately 45-60 seconds, scraping the purée across the base of the pan so it darkens slightly without scorching.

Ready when: The mixture smells strongly aromatic and the tomato purée has changed from bright red to a slightly darker brick-red colour.
Corrective actions
  • If: The garlic or spices begin catching or smelling bitter.
    Do: Immediately proceed to the next step and add the canned tomatoes to stop further browning.
Step 3

Reduce the shakshuka sauce

Tomato sauce
Heat source: gas_hobSetting: mediumTime: 5–7 min

Add the canned tomatoes, 1 tsp red wine vinegar, 1/4 tsp fine salt and 1/4 tsp black pepper. Stir thoroughly and bring to a lively simmer. Cook uncovered over medium heat for approximately 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally and scraping the base of the pan.

Ready when: The sauce has visibly thickened, a spatula drawn through the centre leaves a trail that remains open for 1-2 seconds, and there is no watery liquid separating around the edges.
Corrective actions
  • If: The sauce thickens and catches before the tomatoes have lost their raw canned flavour.
    Do: Reduce the heat and add 1 tbsp water, then continue simmering.
  • If: The sauce remains watery after 7 minutes.
    Do: Increase the heat slightly and continue uncovered until the stated trail test is achieved.
Step 4

Wilt the spinach

Vegetables
Heat source: gas_hobSetting: mediumTime: 1–2 min

Add the spinach in 2 additions, folding each portion into the hot sauce before adding the next. Cook for approximately 1-2 minutes until all of the spinach has collapsed into the sauce.

Ready when: The spinach is fully wilted and evenly distributed, while the sauce remains thick rather than watery.
Corrective actions
  • If: The spinach releases enough water to loosen the sauce noticeably.
    Do: Continue cooking uncovered for another 1-2 minutes until the sauce returns to the previous thick consistency.
Step 5

Add and set the eggs

Eggs
Heat source: gas_hobSetting: low-mediumTime: 5–7 min

Reduce the heat to low-medium. Make 2 wells in the sauce and crack 1 egg into each well. Pour the 200 g liquid egg whites evenly into the spaces between the eggs rather than directly over the yolks. Cover the pan and cook for approximately 5-7 minutes.

Ready when: All egg white, including the added liquid egg white, is fully opaque and set with no translucent patches; the whole-egg yolks may remain soft if preferred.
Corrective actions
  • If: The tomato sauce begins bubbling aggressively around the eggs.
    Do: Reduce the heat so the eggs set gently rather than becoming tough at the base.
  • If: The egg whites remain translucent after 7 minutes.
    Do: Keep the pan covered and continue cooking in 1-minute increments until all white is opaque.
Step 6

Toast the wholemeal bread

Toast
Heat source: toasterSetting: mediumTime: 2–4 min

While the eggs are setting, toast the 2 slices of wholemeal bread until crisp at the surface and golden at the edges while remaining tender inside.

Ready when: The toast is hot, crisp and evenly browned without scorched edges.
Corrective actions
  • If: The bread is browning too quickly.
    Do: Stop the toaster early rather than allowing the edges to burn.
Step 7

Warm the cottage cheese

Finishing
Heat source: gas_hobSetting: lowTime: 1 min

Once the egg whites are fully set, remove the lid and spoon the cottage cheese in small dollops across the sauce, keeping the egg yolks visible. Cover again for approximately 1 minute over low heat, just until the cottage cheese is warmed but still visibly curded.

Ready when: The cottage cheese is warm and creamy while retaining distinct curds, and the eggs remain correctly cooked.
Corrective actions
  • If: The sauce begins bubbling hard after the cottage cheese is added.
    Do: Turn off the heat and allow the residual pan heat to warm the cottage cheese.
Serving

Divide the shakshuka between two warm bowls or shallow plates so each person receives 1 whole egg, approximately half of the extra egg white, vegetables, tomato sauce and cottage cheese. Serve each portion immediately with 1 slice of crisp wholemeal toast for scooping.

Storage & reheating

Refrigerator: 1 days

The completed shakshuka is best eaten immediately. If necessary, cool leftovers promptly and refrigerate in a sealed container for up to 1 day. Store toast separately so it does not soften. For make-ahead preparation, the mushroom, spinach and tomato base may be prepared up to 2 days in advance and refrigerated; reheat it until piping hot before adding fresh eggs and cottage cheese.

Reheating

  • Hob: place leftover shakshuka in a covered frying or sauté pan over low heat with 1 tbsp water and heat gently until piping hot throughout, avoiding prolonged heating that will toughen the eggs.
  • Oven: cover in a suitable ovenproof dish and heat at approximately 170°C until piping hot throughout.
  • Toast fresh wholemeal bread separately immediately before serving.
