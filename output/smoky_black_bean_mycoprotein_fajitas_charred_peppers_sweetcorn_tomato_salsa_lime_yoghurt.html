Smoky Black Bean & Mycoprotein Fajitas with Charred Peppers, Sweetcorn, Tomato Salsa and Lime Yoghurt

Recipe info

Smoky, deeply browned mycoprotein and black beans with charred peppers and sweetcorn, served in warm wholemeal fajita wraps with a sharp fresh tomato salsa and cooling lime yoghurt.

Mise en place

Prep 1

Drain the black beans, rinse them thoroughly under cold running water and leave them to drain until no free water remains. Measure 100 g frozen sweetcorn and keep it frozen until cooking.

Ready when: The beans are well drained rather than wet, and the sweetcorn is measured.

Prep 2

Halve the peppers, remove the stems, seeds and white membranes, then cut the flesh into strips approximately 8 mm wide. Peel the red onion and dice it into approximately 5 mm pieces; reserve half for the salsa and half for cooking. Peel and finely grate or crush the 2 garlic cloves.

Ready when: The peppers are evenly sliced, the onion is divided into two equal portions and the garlic is finely prepared.

Prep 3

Dice the tomatoes into approximately 8 mm pieces. Finely chop the coriander leaves and tender stems. Finely grate the zest from half of the lime, then halve the lime and squeeze the juice.

Ready when: The tomatoes are diced without excessive free liquid, the coriander is chopped, and the lime zest and juice are ready.

Prep 4

Measure 2 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp ground cumin, 1 tsp dried oregano and 1/2 tsp mild chilli powder together. Keep the 3/4 tsp fine salt and 1/2 tsp black pepper available separately for staged seasoning.

Ready when: The spice mixture and seasonings are measured before cooking begins.

Method

Step 1

Make the tomato salsa

Cold accompaniments
Time: 2–3 min

Combine the diced tomatoes, the reserved half of the diced red onion and approximately half of the chopped coriander. Add approximately half of the lime juice and 1/4 tsp fine salt. Fold together gently and leave at room temperature while the filling cooks.

Ready when: The salsa tastes fresh, lightly salted and distinctly lime-sharp without a pool of liquid in the bottom of the bowl.
Corrective actions
  • If: The salsa releases a large amount of watery tomato juice.
    Do: Spoon off the excess liquid before serving so the tortillas do not become soggy.
Step 2

Make the lime yoghurt

Cold accompaniments
Time: 1–2 min

Stir the 120 g Greek yoghurt with the lime zest and most of the remaining lime juice. Reserve a small amount of lime juice for final adjustment of the hot filling.

Ready when: The yoghurt is smooth, cooling and clearly lime-scented while remaining thick enough to spoon onto the fajitas.
Corrective actions
  • If: The yoghurt becomes too loose after adding lime juice.
    Do: Do not add any more lime juice; reserve the remainder for the filling.
Step 3

Warm the tortilla wraps

Tortillas
Heat source: gas_hobSetting: medium-highTime: 2–3 min

Place the dry frying pan over medium-high heat. Warm the tortilla wraps one at a time for approximately 15-20 seconds per side, just until pliable and lightly toasted in small spots. Stack them immediately and cover with a clean plate or clean tea towel while the filling cooks.

Ready when: The tortillas are warm and flexible with a few light toasted spots but are not crisp or dry.
Corrective actions
  • If: A tortilla stiffens or develops dark spots within a few seconds.
    Do: Reduce the heat before warming the remaining tortillas.
Step 4

Char the peppers and onion

Fajita filling
Heat source: gas_hobSetting: highTime: 6–8 min

Increase the pan to high heat. Add 1 tsp rapeseed oil, then add the pepper strips and the half of the red onion reserved for cooking. Spread them over the pan and leave undisturbed for approximately 1 minute before stirring. Continue cooking for approximately 6-8 minutes, stirring only every 45-60 seconds so the vegetables can develop dark charred edges while softening.

Ready when: The peppers are tender but retain some bite, with distinct dark blistered edges, and the onion is softened and browned.
Corrective actions
  • If: The vegetables begin blackening before they soften.
    Do: Reduce the heat to medium-high and stir more frequently.
  • If: The vegetables release liquid and steam without colouring.
    Do: Keep the heat high, spread them into a single layer and avoid stirring until the visible moisture has evaporated.
Step 5

Brown the mycoprotein

Fajita filling
Heat source: gas_hobSetting: medium-highTime: 5–6 min

Push the vegetables towards the edge of the pan. Add the remaining 1 tsp rapeseed oil to the centre, followed by the mycoprotein pieces. Spread the pieces over the hottest area and cook for approximately 5-6 minutes, turning occasionally, until they are piping hot throughout and have developed clear golden-brown patches.

Ready when: The mycoprotein is piping hot and visibly browned rather than pale or wet.
Corrective actions
  • If: The mycoprotein starts sticking before it browns.
    Do: Reduce the heat slightly and allow the pieces another 30-60 seconds before attempting to turn them.
  • If: Liquid collects in the pan.
    Do: Continue cooking uncovered at medium-high heat until the liquid has evaporated before adding the spices.
Step 6

Bloom the smoky spices

Fajita filling
Heat source: gas_hobSetting: mediumTime: 0.5–0.75 min

Reduce the heat to medium. Add the garlic and the measured smoked-paprika spice mixture. Stir continuously for approximately 30-45 seconds, coating the vegetables and mycoprotein thoroughly.

Ready when: The garlic and spices smell strongly aromatic and smoky but have not darkened or scorched.
Corrective actions
  • If: The spices begin catching or smelling bitter.
    Do: Immediately proceed to the next step and add the beans and sweetcorn to cool the pan.
Step 7

Finish with black beans and sweetcorn

Fajita filling
Heat source: gas_hobSetting: medium-highTime: 4–5 min

Add the drained black beans and frozen sweetcorn. Add 1/2 tsp fine salt and 1/2 tsp black pepper. Cook over medium-high heat for approximately 4-5 minutes, folding everything together, until the beans and sweetcorn are piping hot and any surface moisture has evaporated. Remove from the heat, fold through the remaining coriander and add the reserved lime juice a little at a time.

Ready when: The filling is hot, glossy rather than wet, smoky and savoury, with distinct charred vegetables and enough lime acidity to keep the beans and mycoprotein tasting fresh.
Corrective actions
  • If: The filling looks wet enough to soak the tortillas.
    Do: Continue cooking uncovered for 1-2 minutes until excess moisture has evaporated.
  • If: The filling tastes heavy or flat after the stated seasoning.
    Do: Add the remaining reserved lime juice before adding any further salt.
  • If: The filling becomes dry before the beans and sweetcorn are fully hot.
    Do: Add 1 tbsp water, toss through and continue heating briefly.
Serving

Give each person 2 warm small wholemeal tortillas. Divide the smoky black-bean and mycoprotein filling evenly between them, then spoon over the fresh tomato salsa and lime yoghurt. Serve immediately while the filling is hot, the salsa is fresh and the tortillas remain pliable.

Storage & reheating

Refrigerator: 2 days

For leftovers, cool the cooked fajita filling promptly and refrigerate it separately from the tortillas, salsa and lime yoghurt in sealed containers. Refrigerate the salsa and yoghurt separately and use within 2 days. Keep unused tortillas sealed according to their pack instructions. Do not assemble fajitas before storage because the tortillas will become soggy.

Reheating

  • Hob: reheat the filling in a frying pan over medium heat, stirring regularly, until piping hot throughout; add 1 tbsp water if it begins to dry.
  • Air fryer: place the filling in a suitable heatproof dish, cover loosely with foil and heat at approximately 170°C until piping hot, stirring once during reheating.
  • Warm the tortillas separately in a dry frying pan for approximately 15-20 seconds per side before serving.
