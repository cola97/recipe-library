Red Lentil, Spinach & Cauliflower Dal with Brown Basmati Rice and Cucumber Raita

A high-protein red lentil dal with browned cauliflower, wilted spinach and a deeply aromatic cumin, ginger, garlic and tomato base, served with nutty brown basmati rice and a cool lemon-mint cucumber raita. The cauliflower is roasted separately for caramelised edges before being folded through the finished dal so it retains texture.

Mise en place

Prep 1

Inspect 120 g dried red lentils for foreign material. Place in the fine colander or sieve and rinse under cold running water while agitating the lentils until the runoff becomes substantially clearer. Drain thoroughly.

Ready when: The lentils are clean, fully drained and ready for cooking.

Prep 2

Place 70 g brown basmati rice in a sieve and rinse under cold running water while agitating the grains until the runoff becomes substantially clearer. Shake thoroughly to remove excess water.

Ready when: The rice is thoroughly rinsed and drained.

Prep 3

Remove the cauliflower leaves and trim away the tough base. Wash and dry the usable head. Cut florets into pieces approximately 30-40 mm across. Peel tough outer skin from the tender central stem where required and cut the usable stem into pieces of similar cooking thickness. Weigh approximately 400 g prepared cauliflower.

Ready when: Approximately 400 g cauliflower is clean, dry and cut into evenly sized roasting pieces.

Prep 4

Peel the onion, trim the root and stem ends and dice the usable flesh into approximately 4 mm pieces. Wash the ginger, peel only if the skin is tough and finely grate 20 g usable flesh. Separate 3 garlic cloves, peel, trim the root ends and finely grate.

Ready when: The onion is finely diced and the ginger and garlic are finely grated and separately identifiable.

Prep 5

Inspect the spinach and coriander. Wash if required, then dry thoroughly. Remove damaged spinach leaves. Remove coarse lower coriander stems and chop the leaves and tender stems into approximately 5 mm pieces.

Ready when: The spinach is clean and dry, and 15 g coriander is prepared for finishing.

Prep 6

Wash and dry the cucumber. Cut approximately half to provide 160 g. Coarsely grate the cucumber using the box grater. Transfer it to a sieve or clean hands and squeeze gently until obvious free water stops running; do not crush it completely dry. Wash and dry the mint, remove coarse stems and finely chop 10 g. Weigh 250 g Greek yoghurt.

Ready when: The cucumber is coarsely grated and no longer releasing substantial free water, the mint is finely chopped and the yoghurt is measured.

Prep 7

Squeeze 30 ml lemon juice and remove all seeds. Measure 12 g rapeseed oil, allocating 4 g to the cauliflower and 8 g to the dal. Measure 1 tsp cumin seeds, 1.5 tsp ground coriander, 0.5 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp garam masala, 0.25 tsp chilli powder, 0.5 tsp ground cumin and 0.5 tsp fine salt, allocating the salt as specified in the ingredient notes. Measure 200 g canned chopped tomatoes and 550 ml water.

Ready when: All remaining ingredients are accurately measured and allocated to their intended components.

Method

Step 1

Cook the brown basmati rice

Brown basmati rice
Heat source: Rice cookerSetting: Brown rice programmeTime: 30–45 min

Transfer the rinsed 70 g brown basmati rice to the rice cooker. Add the quantity of water specified by the rice cooker's brown-rice markings or manufacturer guidance for this quantity. Select the brown-rice programme where available and start the cycle. Keep the lid closed during cooking.

Ready when: The rice is tender with slight chew, contains no hard raw core and has no free water remaining at the base.
Corrective actions
  • If: The rice remains firm after the cycle and no free water remains.
    Do: Add 30 ml hot water, close the lid and continue cooking or holding hot for 5-10 minutes before reassessing.
  • If: The grains are tender but visibly wet.
    Do: Leave the lid open on keep-warm for several minutes until excess moisture evaporates.
Step 2

Preheat the air fryer

Roasted cauliflower
Heat source: Air fryerSetting: 200°CTemperature: 200°CTime: 3–5 min

Preheat the air fryer to 200°C and allow it to complete its preheat cycle before loading.

Ready when: The air fryer has reached 200°C.
Corrective actions
  • If: The appliance has not reached temperature after 5 minutes.
    Do: Confirm the setting and continue preheating for an additional 2 minutes.
Step 3

Season and roast the cauliflower

Roasted cauliflower
Heat source: Air fryerSetting: 200°CTemperature: 200°CTime: 12–16 min

Place the prepared 400 g cauliflower in the mixing bowl. Add 4 g rapeseed oil, 0.125 tsp fine salt and 0.5 tsp of the measured ground coriander. Toss until all pieces have a thin, even coating. Transfer to the preheated air fryer in as loose a layer as practical and roast at 200°C, shaking thoroughly after approximately 6 minutes.

Ready when: The cauliflower is tender through the thickest stem sections but still structured, with clearly browned and lightly charred edges on multiple florets.
Corrective actions
  • If: Edges darken substantially before the stems become tender.
    Do: Reduce the temperature to 185°C and continue in 2-minute intervals.
  • If: The cauliflower is tender but remains pale.
    Do: Increase airflow by spreading the pieces more widely and continue at 200°C for 2-3 minutes.
Step 4

Bloom cumin and brown the onion

Dal base
Heat source: Gas hobSetting: MediumTime: 7–10 min

Place the sauté pan over medium gas heat. Add the remaining 8 g rapeseed oil. When the oil is fluid and shimmering lightly, add 1 tsp cumin seeds and stir for approximately 20-30 seconds until fragrant. Add the finely diced onion and approximately 0.125 tsp fine salt. Cook while stirring every 30-60 seconds.

Ready when: The onion is fully softened and shows clear golden-brown colour at the edges, while the cumin smells nutty and aromatic rather than burnt.
Corrective actions
  • If: The cumin seeds darken rapidly or smell burnt.
    Do: Reduce the heat immediately and add the onion to lower the pan temperature.
  • If: The onion browns before becoming tender.
    Do: Reduce the flame and continue cooking more slowly until fully soft.
  • If: The onion remains pale after 10 minutes.
    Do: Increase the heat slightly and stir less frequently, allowing brief contact with the pan surface.
Step 5

Cook the ginger, garlic and ground spices

Dal base
Heat source: Gas hobSetting: Medium-lowTime: 1–2 min

Reduce the heat to medium-low. Add the 20 g grated ginger and 3 grated garlic cloves. Stir continuously for approximately 45 seconds until strongly aromatic. Add the remaining 1 tsp ground coriander, 0.5 tsp turmeric and 0.25 tsp mild chilli powder. Stir continuously so the spices contact the oil-coated aromatics without remaining stationary on the pan.

Ready when: The ginger and garlic smell cooked rather than raw, and the spices are highly aromatic without dark or scorched patches.
Corrective actions
  • If: The garlic or spices begin catching on the pan.
    Do: Immediately proceed to the tomato addition in Step 6 and scrape the pan thoroughly as the tomato liquid is introduced.
Step 6

Reduce the tomato-spice base

Dal
Heat source: Gas hobSetting: MediumTime: 4–7 min

Add the measured 200 g chopped tomatoes to the pan. Increase the heat to medium. Stir and scrape the pan base to release any browned spice residue. Simmer uncovered, stirring regularly.

Ready when: The tomatoes have reduced into a thick, cohesive masala with little free watery liquid and a deeper red-orange colour.
Corrective actions
  • If: The tomato mixture begins sticking before it thickens.
    Do: Reduce the heat and add 15 ml of the measured dal water, then continue cooking.
  • If: The mixture remains watery after 7 minutes.
    Do: Increase the heat slightly and continue stirring until the stated thickened endpoint is reached.
Step 7

Simmer the red lentils

Dal
Heat source: Gas hobSetting: Low-medium simmerTime: 15–22 min

Bring the measured 550 ml water to the boil in the kettle. Add the rinsed red lentils to the tomato-spice base and stir for approximately 20 seconds. Pour in the 550 ml hot water and stir thoroughly, scraping the pan base. Bring to a visible simmer, then reduce the gas flame to low-medium and cook uncovered or partially covered, stirring every 3-4 minutes.

Ready when: The lentils are completely tender with no chalky centre and have partially broken down into a thick spoonable dal that slowly levels after stirring.
Corrective actions
  • If: The lentils remain firm but the mixture becomes too thick to stir freely.
    Do: Add 50 ml hot water and continue simmering until the lentils pass the tenderness endpoint.
  • If: The dal remains very watery after the lentils become tender.
    Do: Increase the heat to medium and simmer uncovered while stirring frequently until the required thick spoonable consistency is reached.
  • If: The dal begins sticking to the pan base.
    Do: Reduce the heat immediately, scrape the base thoroughly and add 30-50 ml hot water if required.
Step 8

Prepare the cucumber raita

Cucumber raita
Time: 2–4 min

Place 250 g Greek yoghurt in the small bowl. Add the drained grated cucumber, 10 g chopped mint, 0.5 tsp ground cumin, 10 ml lemon juice and 0.125 tsp fine salt. Fold until evenly combined. Refrigerate until plating.

Ready when: The raita is thick and spoonable with cucumber and mint evenly dispersed and no pool of watery liquid forming around the edges.
Corrective actions
  • If: The raita becomes watery immediately after mixing.
    Do: Drain off any obvious free liquid and confirm that the remaining grated cucumber has been adequately squeezed.
Step 9

Wilt the spinach and finish the dal

Dal finishing
Heat source: Gas hobSetting: LowTime: 3–5 min

When the lentils have passed the tenderness and consistency endpoint, add the 160 g spinach in 2-3 batches, folding each addition until wilted before adding the next. Add the roasted cauliflower, reserving a few browned florets for visible garnish if desired within the specified quantity. Fold in 1 tsp garam masala, 20 ml lemon juice, 15 g chopped coriander and the remaining approximately 0.125 tsp fine salt.

Ready when: The spinach is fully wilted, the cauliflower is coated but retains its shape, the dal remains thick and spoonable, and the final flavour is distinctly savoury, aromatic and acidic without raw spice character.
Corrective actions
  • If: The dal becomes too thick after the spinach and cauliflower are added.
    Do: Add hot water in 20 ml increments and fold after each addition until the dal returns to a spoonable consistency.
  • If: The dal tastes flat despite adequate salt.
    Do: Allow the measured lemon and garam masala to disperse for 30 seconds before reassessing; do not add unlisted seasoning.
  • If: The cauliflower begins breaking apart excessively.
    Do: Stop vigorous stirring and use broad folding motions only.
Step 10

Fluff the brown basmati rice

Rice
Heat source: Rice cookerSetting: Keep warm or offTime: 2–3 min

When the rice cooker completes and the rice passes its tenderness endpoint, open the lid and gently fluff the grains from the base upward. Leave the lid open for approximately 2 minutes to release excess steam.

Ready when: The rice grains are separate, tender and free from visible surface water.
Corrective actions
  • If: The rice is compacted.
    Do: Lift and separate the grains gently rather than stirring vigorously.
Step 11

Divide the complete dinner

Final portioning
Time: 2–4 min

Divide the cooked brown basmati rice approximately equally between 2 bowls. Divide the finished dal equally over or alongside the rice. Divide the cucumber raita equally, approximately half per serving, placing it beside rather than directly into the hottest part of the dal.

Ready when: Both bowls contain approximately equal quantities of rice, dal, roasted cauliflower, spinach and cucumber raita.
Corrective actions
  • If: One serving is visibly heavier than the other.
    Do: Use the kitchen scales to transfer components until both servings are approximately equal.
Serving

Serve the brown basmati rice as a compact base or side portion, spoon the thick red lentil, spinach and cauliflower dal alongside it, and place the chilled cucumber raita separately so each mouthful can alternate between hot spiced dal and cool yoghurt. Keep any reserved browned cauliflower visible on top for textural contrast.

Storage & reheating

Refrigerator: 3 days

Freezer: 2 months

Cool the dal and rice promptly and store in separate sealed containers at 5°C or below for up to 3 days. Store the raita separately and consume within 2 days. The dal may be frozen for up to 2 months; the raita should not be frozen because the yoghurt and cucumber texture will deteriorate. Reheat cooked components only once and ensure they are steaming hot throughout before serving. Keep refrigerated raita cold and add only after reheating is complete.

Reheating

  • Hob: place the dal in a saucepan with 30-60 ml water per serving and reheat over medium-low heat, stirring frequently, until steaming hot throughout.
  • Hob for rice: place the rice in a small saucepan with 15-30 ml water per serving, cover and heat over low heat until steaming hot throughout, stirring once or twice.
  • Air fryer: roasted cauliflower reserved separately may be reheated at 170°C for approximately 4-6 minutes until hot and lightly crisp before being added back to the reheated dal.
  • From frozen: thaw the dal overnight in the refrigerator before reheating on the hob.
